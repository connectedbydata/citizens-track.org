### Citizens’ Track on AI

---

> Artificial intelligence is already shaping how we live, work, learn and participate in society.  *What would AI look like if communities had a greater say in how it is developed and governed?   What concerns, priorities and opportunities would emerge if people could explore these questions together?* 

The Citizens’ Track on AI is creating opportunities for communities around the world to do just that: bring people together to learn, deliberate and identify shared priorities for the future of AI. We invite community organisations, civil society networks, facilitators and local leaders to organise Community Assemblies on AI, connecting local perspectives and experiences with global conversations on AI governance, including the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance and the 2027 Geneva AI Summit. Join us in bringing community voices into the conversation on the future of AI 

---

## **What is a Community Assembly on AI?**

A **Community Assembly** is a participatory process that brings diverse people together to discuss issues related to AI that matter to their community, explore different perspectives and **collectively identify priorities, solutions and actions.**

**A Community Assembly typically involves:**

* **15–50 participants**, with an emphasis on diversity and inclusion  
* **1–to–2 days** of structured learning and dialogue around AI (approximately 4–16 hours)  
* Accessible **learning materials and facilitated discussions**  
* Opportunities to share individual perspectives, identify common ground and develop collective recommendations or priorities

## **How can you get involved?**

**You do not need to be an AI or deliberation expert to organise an assembly.** We are particularly interested in working with people and organisations who have strong community connections and want to create meaningful opportunities for public participation on AI.

**1. Organise an Assembly** Join the road to Geneva and organise a locally led assembly using the CTAI Community Assembly Toolkit, adapting the process to your community's needs and interests. 

**2. Explore CTAI’s support** Interested in organising an assembly but unsure where to start? Tell us about your community, your experience and the support you might need. We welcome expressions of interest from organisers who would benefit from our team’s technical support: training, facilitation guidance, assistance or financial support. 

**3. Share an existing assembly or initiative** Have you already organised a community dialogue, deliberative process or assembly on AI? We would love to hear about it.  **CTAI** is also documenting existing experiences and lessons through the [**PAVE Case Book**](https://pavecasebook.org/), helping connect independent initiatives and their findings to broader **global AI governance discussions.** 

## **What can CTAI offer?**

Depending on your needs, available resources and the nature of your involvement, CTAI aims to offer:

**Training and technical support**

* Access to the **Community Assembly Toolkit**, other adaptable learning materials, facilitation guidance, dialogue modules and tools for documenting community perspectives.  
* Opportunities for facilitator training, methodological guidance and peer learning to support locally led assemblies.

**Potential financial support**

* Limited **1000 USD micro-grants** may be available for selected Community Assemblies, subject to funding and eligibility. Financial support is not guaranteed, and organisations are welcome to participate using their own resources.

**Pathways to global dialogue**

* Opportunities to contribute community perspectives and findings to CTAI's collective learning and synthesis processes, informing discussions connected to the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance and the 2027 Geneva AI Summit.
