## Co-design Sprint: A toolkit for Community Assemblies on AI

Community Assemblies are events that bring together diverse members of a local community to learn about, discuss and share ideas and actions about a topic. 

Over October 2026, we are running an online **co-design sprint** to build the first version of an open, adaptable **Community Assembly Toolkit on AI**. The toolkit will provide practical guidance, deliberation modules, and feedback tools to help local organizers across the world facilitate informed community dialogues on AI that connect directly to local and global governance processes. 

This is a critical component in our goal to support hundreds of community assemblies across the world in the run up to the next UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance next year. 

We're inviting a cohort of 10–15 practitioners, facilitators, educators, and deliberative designers to collaborate across five weekly 90-minute online sessions alongside asynchronous drafting. You can [read more about the toolkit and process](/toolkit/), or [apply by 25th September to join the co-design sprint](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSetV2DYnT9ZRv1EiU0UPleI6QTQQAPUh3cYO6YG2N3-q0Ul1g/viewform?usp=dialog){:target="_blank"}.

## Funding Foundations and Growing Governance

The design phase of the Citizens' Track on AI has been carried out with in-kind support from Iswe Foundation, Connected by Data, University of Sheffield, Ada Lovelace Institute and a number of other partners, as well as a small start-up mandate from the Swiss Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It has been run as an informal partnership project. 

As we look ahead, we're looking to raise funds to: 

* build a core delivery team supporting community assemblies and creating docking points for public voice; 
* develop a network of regional fellows initiating and supporting community dialogues on AI; 
* provide micro-grants to address gaps in current AI engagement, ensuring all voices are heard; and 
* bring public participants together in moments of transnational deliberation. 

We are working on a number of funding applications at the moment, and in September, along with ISWE Foundation, we are also part of a number of funder roundtables (In San Francisco, and alongside the UN General Assembly meetings in New York) focussed on mobilising wider support into the democratisation of AI governance. 

You can support this work making a [donation to the Citizens' Track on AI](https://donate.stripe.com/aFa9AV5I97vZ8jd8gFc7u00), getting in touch if you could offer a letter of support for one of our upcoming funding applications, or reaching out if you spot an opportunity to explore a joint funding proposal. 

In parallel to fundraising, we've been working to bootstrap the future governance of the Citizens' Track on AI:

* We're aiming to create an unincorporated association to provide project oversight with a small board of partners from across the world;
* We're drafting membership and partnership agreements to set out clear relationships for fiscal hosts of activities, lead partners, supporters, and other collaborators;
* We're working on website updates to include more information about the team and partners involved, funding, governance and activities. 

We're committed to working collaboratively and in the open.
