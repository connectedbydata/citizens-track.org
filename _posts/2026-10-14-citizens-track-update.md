## Taking Public Voice from Local Dialogue to Global Governance

September was an intensive month for the Citizens' Track on AI. Across four co-design workshops, two roundtables, and countless bilateral conversations in San Francisco, Washington DC, and New York alongside the UN General Assembly, we set out to answer two interconnected questions:

1. **How do we make community conversations about AI accessible, grounded, and empowering?**
2. **How do we ensure that what local communities say actually travels upwards to shape industry decisions and multilateral governance?**

As conversations about AI continue to be dominated by industry hype, geopolitical races, and closed-door negotiations, the case for structured, bottom-up public participation has never been clearer or more urgent.

Read on for an overview of what we've learned over the past month, where we are heading next, and how you can get involved.

Each session provided a distinct set of insights into the priority topics, participation methods, and design considerations for productive community dialogues on AI. One common theme was the importance of ending sessions with a sense of the actions that can and should be taken: whether through sharing messages for industry and government stakeholders, or through equipping participants to understand local actions that could be taken, such as: 

* Demanding **public procurement standards** and community audit rights before local governments or school districts buy commercial AI systems;
* Focussing on **environmental transparency and zoning oversight** over data center developments;
* Establishing **redress pathways** so residents can question automated public-service decisions with real human beings;
* Supporting **community-led and public-interest open-source AI tools** and changing the default settings on the AI tools we use;

## Roundtables in San Francisco and New York: Mobilising Resources for Democratic AI

Alongside community workshops, the Citizens' Track on AI partnered with the **Iswe Foundation**, the **Consulate General of Switzerland in San Francisco**, the **Global Partnership for Sustainable Development Data (GPSDD)**, and the **Trust, Accountability and Inclusion Collaborative (TAI)** to convene two roundtables on resources for democratic AI governance.

Held under the Chatham House Rule in San Francisco and at the Ford Foundation Center for Social Justice in New York, these discussions brought together philanthropic foundations, international institutions, and participatory AI practitioners to explore how to advance the field of participatory AI. 

As summarized in our [recent post on mobilizing resources for democratic AI](/2026/10/05/mobilising-resources-for-democratic-ai.html), five key insights framed the discussions:

1. **A vibrant field already exists:** The [Public AI Voice and Engagement (PAVE) Case Book](https://pave.pairs.site) documents over 80 existing participatory AI initiatives across five continents. The blueprint of the Citizens' Track demonstrates viable pathways to connect these localized efforts to international bodies like the UN and the Geneva AI Summit.
2. **Public participation is essential to Public AI:** Funders seeking to build "Public AI" or "AI for Good" cannot succeed through technical development alone. Democratic participation generates public legitimacy, shifts public sector procurement demand, and moves public debate from reactive backlash to proactive design of shared public goods.
3. **Democratic AI is central to broader democratic renewal:** Governing powerful technologies is inseparable from defending democratic culture against corporate concentration and authoritarian capture.
4. **Funders need a collective learning agenda:** Many traditional democracy, human rights, and social justice donors are still determining how to approach AI. Deliberative pilots offer funders and frontline partners a practical space to test "what works" together.
5. **There is an urgent window for rapid resourcing:** Regulatory frameworks and technical standards are crystallizing now. If public participation is not resourced immediately, elite and industry-led settlements will lock in for a generation.

## The Community Assembly Toolkit: October Sprint Underway

Following an open application process, we have kicked off our **October Co-Design Sprint** to create the first version of the **Community Assembly Toolkit on AI**.

A global cohort of nearly 30 practitioners—spanning deliberative design, critical AI literacy, popular education, and grassroots community organizing—is have met three times now to explore the creation of a resource that will ultimately contain: 

* **Facilitator Guidance:** Guidance on venue selection, participant outreach, welcoming diverse community members, and establishing constructive dialogue agreements;
* **Thematic Deliberation Modules:** Plug-and-play activities covering AI 101/Foundations, AI Safety, Language and Diversity, AI Infrastructures, and AI Governance. 
* **Feedback and Action Frameworks:** Structured templates to capture local community demands and feed them into municipal governance, national policy consultations, and international summits.

We'll share news of the first release of the toolkit to all mailing list subscribers in November.
